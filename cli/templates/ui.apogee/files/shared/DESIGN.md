# Apogee · a weekly letter about the night sky

Build the first screen of "Apogee", one weekly letter about space for people who are busy. The page is one surreal photograph that is alive, with a headline, an email field and a glass footer panel.

**Pictures (generate, photographic, fine film grain, no text).** (1) Sky: deep teal at the top going to a warm band at a dead-level horizon. (2) A tidal flat at dusk whose wet sand mirrors the sky. (3) A dark ridge in the lower third with a small figure beside a telescope on a tripod, as a cut-out. (4) Two wide strips of low mist. (5) A star field. (6) A band texture for the planet (soft cloud belts, cream, rust and slate), wide enough to wrap around a sphere.

**The planet is drawn in code.** A small canvas draws a sphere from the band texture: the bands wrap round the limb and rotate, the night side falls into the colour of the sky, and a soft glow sits on the lit limb. Rings are drawn as ellipses that catch the light and carry the planet's shadow. The planet is half-set behind the horizon and finished by its reflection in the sand.

**Words.** Top left chip "NO. 214 IS OUT"; top right "The archive" and "Read this week's letter". Headline, two lines, light grotesk, about 4.8% of the width: "One letter a week. / Worth looking up for." Glass field "Your email" with a darker glass pill "SEND IT TO ME"; under it "Sundays, 7 minutes, no adverts. 61,000 readers." Glass panel at the bottom: mark + "APOGEE"; "What is in the sky this week, and why it matters, in plain words."; columns Read (This week · The archive · Start here · Sky calendar), Letter (Who writes it · Sources · Corrections), Help (Contact · Privacy · Unsubscribe); "Next issue: Sunday 23 November"; three small glyph keys (feed, envelope, arrow).

**Motion.** From the first frame the planet turns, two moons go round it (behind the disc, then in front), the stars wheel slowly about the horizon, a meteor crosses every few seconds, the two mists flow along the water at different speeds, and the grain keeps moving. The pointer parts the layers; with no pointer the view drifts by itself. Type an email and press the pill: a point of light leaves the button, rises on an arc and joins the rings, and the reader count goes up by one. With reduced motion, one still frame.

**Phone.** Planet in the upper half with the headline over it, the field, and the glass panel as a sheet with one column.
